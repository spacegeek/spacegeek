## ConSERVA

My primary project is **ConSERVA**, a cross-domain research and software initiative exploring how records, physical and digital objects, observations, assertions, evidence, relationships, decisions and revisions can be represented without discarding uncertainty or historical context.

ConSERVA is intended to support uses ranging from personal collections and small workshops to industrial operations, archives, laboratories, businesses and institutions. Its development is guided by established standards, research and practical operating experience where appropriate while identifying areas in which current systems or models remain incomplete.

The project is particularly concerned with:

* Preserving the history of records, objects, claims and decisions
* Distinguishing observations from conclusions
* Representing uncertain, disputed or evolving identity
* Maintaining traceability between evidence and assertions
* Retaining operational and institutional knowledge
* Supporting standards-aware workflows without forcing every domain into one model
* Providing understandable and reproducible retrieval
* Evaluating when artificial intelligence or language models are useful, unnecessary, unreliable or inappropriate

## Current direction

My current work focuses on establishing the conceptual, technical and evidentiary foundations of ConSERVA. This includes studying digital-preservation frameworks, provenance models, archival practice, terminology systems, operational recordkeeping, information retrieval, software architecture and the evaluation of AI and language-model systems.

The goal is not to create another isolated database or documentation tool. The goal is to investigate how durable systems can preserve what is known, what is uncertain, how conclusions were reached, what changed and why those changes matter.
